\section{Introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) have shown strong capabilities in synthesizing scientific information and generating answers, predictions, and explanations. However, they can also produce hallucinated or scientifically invalid claims, particularly when correctness depends on scientific facts, quantitative relations, logical constraints, or combinations of multiple scientific principles. Existing verification methods largely rely on retrieval or end-to-end neural judgments, which can identify simple factual errors but are less reliable when validating a claim requires explicit reasoning over scientific principles. This project proposes neural-symbolic methods for verifying and refining LLM-generated scientific claims by combining the flexibility of neural models with explicit representations and execution of scientific facts, rules, and constraints.
